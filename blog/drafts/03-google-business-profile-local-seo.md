# Google Business Profile Tips to Win More Local Customers

When someone searches "physiotherapist near me", "best pav bhaji in Vadodara" or "AC repair Gotri", Google usually shows a map with three businesses before any website. That box is called the local pack, and those three spots get a large share of the calls, direction requests and visits.

What decides who appears there? Mostly, your Google Business Profile. It is free, it is powerful and most small businesses use only a fraction of it.

Here is how to set it up properly and keep it working for you.

## How Google ranks local businesses

Google has said that local results are based mainly on three factors:

1. **Relevance:** how well your profile matches what the person searched for
2. **Distance:** how far your business is from the searcher or the location in the search
3. **Prominence:** how well known and trusted your business is, based on reviews, links, mentions and overall web presence

You cannot change distance. You can strongly improve relevance and prominence, and that is where the work goes.

## Step 1: Claim and verify your profile

Search for your business on Google Maps. If a listing exists, claim it. If not, create one at business.google.com.

Verification methods include video verification, phone, email or postcard, depending on your business. Google may ask you to record a short video showing your signboard, workplace and proof of management, so keep your signage visible and documents handy.

## Step 2: Choose the right categories

Your **primary category** is one of the strongest relevance signals. Pick the most specific one that describes your main business. "Dental clinic" is better than "Medical clinic" if dentistry is what you do.

Then add a few relevant **secondary categories**. A salon might add "Bridal makeup artist" and "Nail salon" if it truly offers those services. Do not add categories for services you do not provide; it confuses customers and can hurt trust.

## Step 3: Fill in every detail

An incomplete profile looks neglected. Complete:

- business name exactly as it appears on your signboard (do not add keywords to the name; that breaks Google's guidelines and can get the listing suspended)
- address and service area
- phone number and website
- opening hours, including special hours for Diwali, Eid, Christmas and other holidays
- business description of up to 750 characters, written for customers, mentioning your main services and area naturally
- attributes such as wheelchair access, women-owned, online appointments or free Wi-Fi where true

Use the same name, address and phone number everywhere online. Consistency is a quiet but important ranking signal.

## Step 4: List your products and services

The Services and Products sections let you show exactly what you offer, with descriptions and prices. They help Google match your profile to specific searches.

A tuition centre, for example, can list "Class 10 Maths coaching", "Class 12 Science coaching" and "JEE foundation batch" as separate services, each with a short description.

## Step 5: Add real photos, regularly

Profiles with genuine photos tend to get more engagement. Add:

- the exterior, so people recognise you when they arrive
- the interior, team and work in progress
- products, before-and-after results (with permission) and happy customers (with permission)

Use your own photos, not stock images. Short videos of your space or a service being done work well too. Add new photos every month so the profile feels alive.

## Step 6: Build a steady flow of honest reviews

Reviews influence both ranking and the customer's decision. The keys are volume, freshness, quality and your replies.

How to get more reviews ethically:

- ask every satisfied customer, in person, at the right moment
- share your direct review link through WhatsApp or SMS after the service
- place a QR code at the billing counter
- make the request simple: "It would really help us if you shared your experience on Google"

What not to do:

- buy reviews or post fake ones
- offer discounts or gifts in exchange for reviews
- ask only happy customers through a filter while steering unhappy ones elsewhere

Google's policies prohibit fake and incentivised reviews, and it removes them and can restrict profiles that use them.

**Reply to every review.** Thank people by name for positive reviews. For negative ones, stay calm, acknowledge the issue, and offer to resolve it offline. Future customers read your replies as closely as the reviews.

## Step 7: Post updates

Google Business Profile posts let you share offers, events and news. They will not transform your ranking on their own, but they show activity and give searchers more reasons to choose you.

Post once a week if you can. Ideas: a new service, a festive offer, a customer tip, a workshop or a behind-the-scenes photo.

## Step 8: Answer questions and enable messaging

Check the questions and answers section of your profile. Answer existing questions and keep information accurate. Where available, enable messaging or add your WhatsApp link so people can contact you in the way they prefer.

## Step 9: Support your profile with your website

Your website and profile work together. Make sure your site:

- has a clear contact page with the same name, address and phone number
- has service pages that mention your city and areas served naturally
- includes LocalBusiness schema markup
- embeds a Google Map of your location
- loads fast on mobile

If you serve several areas, create genuinely useful pages for each one. Avoid copying the same page and only changing the area name.

## Step 10: Track your results

Your profile's Performance section shows how many people viewed your profile, called you, asked for directions or clicked to your website, and which searches found you.

Review these numbers every month. If calls are rising but website clicks are flat, your website may need work. If searches for a service you offer are low, add it to your services list and description.

Google's AI answers and tools such as ChatGPT increasingly draw on business profiles, reviews and directory data when answering local questions. Your Google Business Profile is often your first impression, and it costs nothing. Fill it out completely, keep it accurate, add real photos, earn honest reviews and reply to them. Do these consistently for three months and most local businesses see a clear improvement in calls and visits.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Google Business Profile free?**
Yes. Creating and managing a Google Business Profile is completely free.

**How long does it take to rank on Google Maps?**
It varies by competition and location. Many businesses see improvement within one to three months of fully optimising their profile and building reviews consistently.

**Can I add keywords to my business name on Google?**
No. Your business name should match your real-world name. Adding extra keywords is against Google's guidelines and can lead to suspension.

**Can I have a profile if I do not have a shop?**
Yes. Service-area businesses, such as plumbers or home tutors, can hide their address and show the areas they serve instead.
